# Light sidebar portals — UX Research Agent 2

**Checked October 29, 2026 · 15 references · data, administration and AI workspaces**

The current brief is a **light, modern workspace with a left sidebar and enough room for settings, filters and inspection**. Cal.com's starter supplies the desired restraint; Linear supplies a useful model for product depth. Framework choice is unrestricted. These are references for composition and interaction, not proposals to adopt the products, frameworks or their backend architectures.

## Open these five first

1. **[Attio navigation](https://attio.com/help/reference/attio-101/introduction-to-navigating-attio)** — the closest overall match for a quiet, populated light workspace: compact navigation, grouped objects, search and view controls. [Open the exact light screen](https://a.storyblok.com/f/234930/626x921/27b93190df/attio-sidebar.png).
2. **[Twenty layout](https://docs.twenty.com/getting-started/core-concepts/layout)** — an especially useful source-list/inspector reference with a restrained gray sidebar. [Open the exact light screen](https://mintcdn.com/twenty/nHykqzejUXmkEL88/images/user-guide/home/main-layout.png).
3. **[Dify's July 2026 navigation update](https://www.dify.ai/blog/dify-s-official-cli-difyctl-ships)** — stronger visual personality through pale surfaces, controlled blue and small colored icons, with substantial application controls. [Light workspace](https://assets.dify.ai/official/blog/2026/07/9b6a6958cb3f7ee1.png) · [light list and inspector](https://assets.dify.ai/official/blog/2026/07/153e4f562824e9da.png).
4. **[Sanity Studio Structure](https://www.sanity.io/docs/studio/structure-tool)** — the best three-pane reference here for category → source list → detailed reading/editing. [Exact light product screen](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/3do82whm/next/25f3e527146f39ec5abdd8549a51d42cd3b6aeb8-3798x2250.png).
5. **[WorkOS Dashboard and Admin Portal documentation](https://workos.com/docs/admin-portal)** — the strongest settings-heavy portal reference: categorized navigation, connection states, precise forms and secondary actions. [Light dashboard settings screen](https://images.workoscdn.com/images/c4b63608-c8fb-4c3f-b0f1-80c287edd47e.png).

The five are aesthetic recommendations, based on the observed screenshots. They are not a measured usability ranking. Plane and NocoDB are also strong layout references; Appsmith, Baserow and PostHog are primarily references for organizing additional controls.

## Evidence and review boundaries

**A — visually confirmed:** the named official screenshot was downloaded from its public publisher page and inspected as an image. All 15 entries below meet this level. Public documentation was read to understand the controls. This does not establish that every screenshot matches the latest deployed application.

**Visual fit** is my judgment of the pictured interface against this brief; **control reference** describes its usefulness for richer workspaces. Those dimensions are deliberately separate. A useful configuration pattern does not make every part of a product an aesthetic target.

Browser surfaces were unavailable, so no live browser walkthrough, keyboard test, responsive test, accessibility audit or logged-in workspace review was performed. No accounts were created, settings changed or third-party UI assets added to Research Scout. Where documentation describes controls beyond the pictured state, that distinction is explicit.

## The 15 references

### 1. Attio — the closest overall light portal

**Open:** [official navigation guide](https://attio.com/help/reference/attio-101/introduction-to-navigating-attio) · [light sidebar and populated companies screen](https://a.storyblok.com/f/234930/626x921/27b93190df/attio-sidebar.png) · [workspace/settings reference](https://attio.com/help/reference/productivity-collaborating/navigating-your-workspace).

**A · Visual fit: high · Control reference: high.** Near-white navigation, subtle selected rows, fine dividers and compact icon-and-label pairs provide density without a dark shell. The screenshot shows grouped Records, Lists and Chats, a workspace menu, persistent search and view controls above a populated table. The guide distinguishes account/workspace settings from current-view settings.

**For Scout:** (1) group Research and Saved shortlist above recent runs, while keeping provider configuration in Settings; (2) use a small view toolbar for filters and sort, with a quiet selected-source state. The pictured table is visual evidence; permissions, shortcut behavior and settings interactions were not exercised.

### 2. Twenty — restrained navigation plus contextual inspection

**Open:** [official layout tour](https://docs.twenty.com/getting-started/core-concepts/layout) · [exact light workspace](https://mintcdn.com/twenty/nHykqzejUXmkEL88/images/user-guide/home/main-layout.png) · [publisher's side-panel screen](https://mintcdn.com/twenty/nHykqzejUXmkEL88/images/user-guide/home/side-panel.png).

**A · Visual fit: high · Control reference: high.** The inspected main-layout screenshot has a muted gray left sidebar, sparse line icons, a white table surface and a compact Filter/Sort/Options strip. Settings and Search are easy to find above workspace objects. The guide documents pinned views, reorderable navigation and a right record panel.

**For Scout:** (1) preserve a clean list with secondary controls in the header; (2) open source details beside the list, retaining the selected row and reading context. Only the main-layout image was visually inspected; the linked side-panel image and its behavior were documented rather than interactively tested.

### 3. Dify — a modern light AI workspace with useful depth

**Open:** [official July 2026 update](https://www.dify.ai/blog/dify-s-official-cli-difyctl-ships) · [new light sidebar/workspace](https://assets.dify.ai/official/blog/2026/07/9b6a6958cb3f7ee1.png) · [logs and inspector](https://assets.dify.ai/official/blog/2026/07/153e4f562824e9da.png) · [control drawer](https://assets.dify.ai/official/blog/2026/07/ca899d3a49e07713.png).

**A · Visual fit: high · Control reference: high.** All three UI images were inspected. The shell combines a pale gray background, white rounded surfaces, a blue selected item and restrained colored application icons. The logs screen adds a date/filter/search toolbar and a large right inspector while the left navigation stays visible. The workflow screen shows a searchable configuration drawer.

**For Scout:** (1) use restrained colored project/provider marks to add identity to an otherwise quiet shell; (2) borrow the list-to-inspector relationship and grouped contextual controls. Workflow editing, templates and the products' AI behavior are outside this review and are not added to Scout's scope.

### 4. Sanity Studio — a strong reading-workbench composition

**Open:** [official Structure tool guide](https://www.sanity.io/docs/studio/structure-tool) · [exact light three-pane screenshot](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/3do82whm/next/25f3e527146f39ec5abdd8549a51d42cd3b6aeb8-3798x2250.png).

**A · Visual fit: high · Control reference: high for inspection.** The image's decorative outer frame is dark, but the application itself is light throughout. Categories occupy the left pane, searchable records occupy the middle and structured details fill the large right pane. Fine boundaries, a strong selected-record highlight and aligned pane headers maintain orientation. Revision/action controls stay near the document.

**For Scout:** (1) compare this category/list/detail hierarchy with Research → source rows → inspector; (2) give title, bibliography, abstract and human notes separate sections with a stable detail toolbar. This is content/workbench evidence, not an inspected global settings area or an endorsement of a three-pane layout at every viewport width.

### 5. WorkOS Dashboard — richer settings with disciplined presentation

**Open:** [official Admin Portal guide](https://workos.com/docs/admin-portal) · [light dashboard settings and modal](https://images.workoscdn.com/images/c4b63608-c8fb-4c3f-b0f1-80c287edd47e.png) · [light organization table and modal](https://images.workoscdn.com/images/48d0efdb-a989-4d52-bf4a-1b0680508439.png) · [public fictional-company demo](https://explore.workos.com/).

**A · Visual fit: high · Control reference: high.** Both dashboard images were inspected. A light labeled sidebar groups Products and Developer destinations; filters sit above the table. Settings use bordered sections, short explanations, clear enabled/disabled labels and focused dialogs. The gray backdrop in these images is modal dimming over a light dashboard.

**For Scout:** (1) put connection readiness and configuration into clear provider-specific sections; (2) explain a setting beside its control, with distinct primary/secondary actions. The public demo was read as a page, not operated; its stateful flows were not tested. The separate SSO setup screen is not the sidebar reference.

### 6. Plane — a useful light alternative to Linear's structure

**Open:** [project settings guide](https://docs.plane.so/projects/project-settings) · [exact light settings screenshot](https://media.docs.plane.so/projects/project-settings.webp) · [navigation customization](https://docs.plane.so/workspaces-and-users/customize-navigation) · [workspace settings map](https://docs.plane.so/workspaces/workspace-settings).

**A · Visual fit: high for composition · Control reference: high.** The inspected screenshot shows labeled global navigation, a second settings column and a broad light form area. Blue selection and primary action provide a restrained accent. **The pictured sample carries a 2024 project date and differs from the current guide's settings categories; treat its precise layout as a dated illustration, not proof of today's deployed interface.**

**For Scout:** (1) separate workspace navigation from settings subsections; (2) retain context with breadcrumbs while showing grouped configuration. Current documentation describes customizable sidebar visibility and separate project/workspace settings; those live controls were not tested.

### 7. Strapi — pale surfaces and modular administration

**Open:** [official admin-panel guide](https://docs.strapi.io/cms/features/admin-panel) · [exact light administration homepage](https://docs.strapi.io/img/assets/admin-homepage/admin-panel-homepage-with-tour.png) · [light logo/settings screen](https://docs.strapi.io/img/assets/settings/settings_custom-logo.png).

**A · Visual fit: medium–high · Control reference: high.** The inspected homepage combines a narrow white icon rail with pale lavender-gray canvas, white bordered sections, dark indigo text and selective purple actions. The guide separately covers appearance, profile and branding configuration. It is a good example of giving a product identity without filling the workspace with saturated backgrounds.

**For Scout:** (1) use very pale tint differences to distinguish shell, work surface and overlays; (2) keep setup/readiness guidance in compact task sections. The homepage was inspected; the linked settings screen was not visually reviewed. Borrow the restrained palette, while keeping labels visible in Scout's main navigation rather than copying icon-only destinations.

### 8. NocoDB — a clear, dense light data workspace

**Open:** [official interface anatomy](https://nocodb.com/docs/product/getting-started/terminologies) · [exact light interface](https://nocodb.com/_next/static/media/layout-overview.1n01j9mwhk9p1.png) · [workspace controls](https://nocodb.com/docs/product/workspaces/create-workspace).

**A · Visual fit: medium–high · Control reference: high.** The inspected image has a white mini rail, labeled project sidebar, pale blue selected view and a separate fields/filter/group/sort toolbar. Compact colored pills distinguish row values without coloring whole rows. Breadcrumbs keep the data context visible.

**For Scout:** (1) place result filters above records and use small status labels for Pending, Included and check outcomes; (2) keep Research and Saved shortlist as explicit destinations, with optional recent-run nesting. This is a data-table control reference; a spreadsheet layout and its many view types are not automatically appropriate for research abstracts. No workspace interaction was tested.

### 9. Langfuse — light operational controls relevant to agent runs

**Open:** [official overview](https://langfuse.com/docs) · [exact light monitors screen](https://langfuse.com/images/docs/monitors-list.png).

**A · Visual fit: medium–high · Control reference: high for operations.** The inspected screenshot has a white labeled sidebar grouped into Observability, Prompt Management and Evaluation, a clear project/environment breadcrumb and a simple list toolbar. Rows distinguish OK, Warning, Alert and No data with text as well as color, with aligned row actions.

**For Scout:** (1) keep operational/run detail navigation secondary to the research journey; (2) model provider/branch states as explicit labeled observations, including unavailable/no-data states. The screenshot's monitors and metrics are sample product content; this research does not validate their values or introduce monitoring dashboards into Increment 1. Settings and live tracing were not exercised.

### 10. Metabase — approachable collections and structured discovery

**Open:** [official Find data tutorial](https://www.metabase.com/learn/metabase-basics/getting-started/find-data) · [exact light collections screen](https://cdn.metabase.com/learn/images/find-data/collection.png) · [general settings reference](https://www.metabase.com/docs/latest/configuring-metabase/settings).

**A · Visual fit: medium · Control reference: high for organization.** The inspected interface uses white/near-white surfaces, blue outline icons, a broad selected collection state and comfortably spaced content sections. Collections and personal areas are easy to distinguish. The tutorial adds search and pinning as ways to keep larger libraries navigable.

**For Scout:** (1) give saved research a visible home with useful labels instead of a large empty dashboard; (2) distinguish personal shortlist state from source/provider observations through grouping and clear wording. General settings were read as documentation, not visually inspected. This reference is warmer and more spacious than Attio; it is not the main density target.

### 11. Directus — powerful configuration hierarchy, mixed chrome

**Open:** [official data-model walkthrough](https://directus.com/docs/getting-started/data-model) · [exact light settings/content screenshot](https://directus.com/docs/img/8a641c77-e13b-4bec-ae54-085a5484cd32.webp).

**A · Visual fit: medium, partial match · Control reference: high.** The inspected screenshot shows white content and pale labeled settings navigation, with purple accents, separated form sections and compact per-field actions. **A narrow dark module rail remains at the far left**, so this is not an all-light-shell recommendation. Configuration destinations include data model, roles, policies, appearance and logs.

**For Scout:** (1) group advanced provider configuration into focused sections; (2) use a primary labeled navigation layer and a contextual settings list when necessary. Copy the hierarchy rather than the dark rail or breadth of administration. Only pictured configuration was reviewed; no data model or permission behavior was tested.

### 12. Payload — a minimal light administration baseline

**Open:** [official admin overview](https://payloadcms.com/docs/admin/overview) · [exact light collection navigation](https://payloadcms.com/images/docs/admin.jpg).

**A · Visual fit: medium for restraint, low for distinctiveness · Control reference: medium.** The inspected image has an entirely white sidebar/work surface, thin boundaries, a prominent page title and simple collection actions. It demonstrates how much navigation can fit without dark chrome. The overview separately documents account theme/locale and extensible administration controls.

**For Scout:** (1) use typography and alignment to establish hierarchy before adding decoration; (2) make collapse/expand explicit when more reading space is useful. This screenshot is deliberately plain, so it is a structural baseline rather than the visual bar for the next concept. Rich forms, settings and responsive behavior were not inspected.

### 13. Baserow — contextual control panels without dark backgrounds

**Open:** [official builder anatomy](https://baserow.io/user-docs/application-builder-overview) · [exact light builder screenshot](https://baserow-backend-production20240528124524339000000001.s3.amazonaws.com/pagedown-uploads/dc58410f-3ff7-41ac-89f7-1551d27c7943/Untitled%201.png) · [application settings](https://baserow.io/user-docs/application-settings).

**A · Visual fit: medium · Control reference: high.** The inspected image has a white canvas, pale compact rail, a top action bar and a separate right panel with General/Style/Events tabs. The documentation distinguishes global settings from selected-element configuration. **The inspected canvas is empty**, so it proves the panel structure, not how a dense populated screen feels.

**For Scout:** (1) keep current-source details in a contextual right pane; (2) separate whole-workspace preferences from per-run scope and per-source decisions. Builder publishing/device controls do not become Scout requirements. The global theme screen and settings behaviors were not visually or interactively tested.

### 14. PostHog — an instructive light navigation-density study

**Open:** [official June 2, 2025 navigation redesign](https://posthog.com/blog/redesigned-nav-menu) · [exact light expanded navigation view](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmukukwp6/image/upload/expanded_light_3df79878f0.png).

**A · Visual fit: medium for structure, lower for current aesthetic brief · Control reference: high.** The inspected screen uses pale warm-gray surfaces, labeled navigation, small differentiated icons, a search field and an expanded table of items. The article explains folders, shortcuts and a searchable product menu as responses to navigation growth. This is **dated 2025 design evidence**, not an inspected current workspace.

**For Scout:** (1) keep the default sidebar short, exposing additional destinations through contextual expansion; (2) surface names, dates and ownership in a wider view when a narrow list becomes insufficient. Do not reproduce the pictured long product catalog or right utility rail in Increment 1. Live shortcuts and keyboard behavior were not tested.

### 15. Appsmith — light settings organization and preview controls

**Open:** [official branding/settings guide](https://docs.appsmith.com/advanced-concepts/branding) · [exact light administration screenshot](https://docs.appsmith.com/img/branding_newbrand.png).

**A · Visual fit: lower for polish · Control reference: high.** The inspected image shows a white settings sidebar with labeled categories, a highlighted active section, a configuration column and multiple preview surfaces. It is visually more utilitarian than the first five; its value is how settings remain navigable and their effects are explained. Publisher annotations are present in the screenshot.

**For Scout:** (1) group preferences, connections and diagnostics into clear settings sections; (2) show meaningful previews for appearance changes if that capability is later introduced. This does not recommend brand management as an Increment 1 feature. The screenshot and guide were reviewed; actual theme propagation, builder interactions and login behavior were not tested.

## Implications for the next Research Scout concept

- **Shell:** start from Attio/Twenty's light labeled sidebar and restrained header. Keep Research and Saved shortlist prominent; put settings and secondary run information in predictable secondary positions. Additional destinations should reflect implemented capabilities.
- **Working area:** compare Sanity's persistent list/detail panes with Dify's right inspector. The source list must remain useful while records progressively arrive, without resetting selection or reading position.
- **Visual identity:** compare Dify's pale blue-gray surfaces and modest icon tiles with Attio's neutral rows. Use one primary accent, clear active states and meaningful small status labels. Do not color every panel or add unrelated charts to create density.
- **Settings depth:** borrow WorkOS's grouped explanations and precise state labels. Keep provider readiness, permissions/access and run scope distinct; separate services do not imply automatically searching or blending all providers.
- **Scope:** source metadata, abstracts, explicit checks and human shortlist decisions remain the current work. These references do not approve workflow builders, collaboration, administration suites, new navigation destinations or a library/framework choice.

Rejected during this pass: Budibase's current datasource imagery was dark; Airtable's pictured review shell had a saturated purple sidebar; the accessible Retool evidence was a light settings popup rather than a full sidebar portal; Helicone's easily accessible visuals were promotional montages without the required full workspace. These products may have useful light states, but they were not counted without suitable observed evidence.
