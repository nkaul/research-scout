# Round 2: three additional design directions

**October 28, 2026 · user-requested further exploration · no design selected**

The user did not accept the first three concepts and requested at least three more, informed by other design frameworks and produced by multiple subagents. Three independent designers each researched a different family of design systems and authored an original standalone HTML screen. The [gallery](index.html) now contains all six, with the new round first. The original three HTML files remain byte-identical.

## What changed in this round

The brief shifted emphasis toward actual research work: a visible reader, stable source selection, clear personal notes and less prominent framing. The new concepts reduce the large mastheads, colored composer areas and repeated notices seen in round 1. Important bibliography remains at least 12px; reading copy is generally 14–16px. Each design uses a concise illustrative-data disclosure, with details secondary to the main working surface.

The designer briefs and findings were coordinated so the new round would contain different compositions. The concepts were not produced by recoloring a shared template. Their sources informed design judgments; they do not install those frameworks or establish that a library would automatically reproduce the appearance.

## Shared research contract

Each sample run retains one explicitly selected discovery provider, OpenAlex. Available sources can be inspected and included while a simulated branch remains pending. Notes and selections belong to the human. Missing abstracts stay missing; an abstract view does not imply acquired full text. There are no invented relevance probabilities, confidence scores, citation metrics or evidence-quality grades.

The samples use different fictional topics to assess a general research workspace. They are not live searches or identical-task usability experiments. All three are offline HTML/CSS/JavaScript with original inline SVG and system fonts. Editable or displayed questions demonstrate composition; replay uses fixed fictional records. State lasts for the page session. Downloads are explicitly marked fictional. Signal Board alone includes a labeled registry simulation; it does not contact a registry or overwrite the source observation.
